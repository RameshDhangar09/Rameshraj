Services

Installation and commissioning of railway machine tools

A machine's accuracy is set on the day it is installed. Our engineers take it from foundation to proven production on your own wheelsets, and hand it over with full documentation.

Why it matters

Accuracy starts with the foundation

An underfloor wheel lathe is part of the building: it sits in a pit beneath the track and must line up with the rails above it. A portal or surface lathe needs a level, stable foundation that will not settle under heavy wheelsets. Small errors at this stage show up for years afterwards as profile deviations, vibration and premature wear.

That is why HYT treats installation as engineering work, not as a delivery task. We issue foundation and pit drawings early in the project, check the site before the machine is shipped, and send engineers who know the machine's design to erect, align and prove it.

The result is a machine that meets its acceptance criteria on your own vehicles and wheelsets, and a team that knows how to run it from the first shift.

Our process

From delivery to handover in seven steps

  1. 01

    Pre-installation planning

    Foundation, pit and rail-support drawings, plus requirements for power, compressed air and lifting, issued to you and your civil contractor.

  2. 02

    Site readiness check

    Our engineers inspect the pit or foundation, levels, anchor positions and cable routes before the machine is dispatched.

  3. 03

    Receipt and placement

    Machine assemblies are inspected on arrival, lifted into position and set on their foundation in the planned sequence.

  4. 04

    Alignment and levelling

    Machines are levelled and, for pit lathes, aligned to the track centre line and rail level before grouting and final fixing.

  5. 05

    Connections

    Electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic and chip-handling systems are connected, and safety interlocks are tested.

  6. 06

    Trials

    Dry runs are followed by machining on your own wheelsets, with results measured against the agreed profile tolerances.

  7. 07

    Training and handover

    Operators and maintainers are trained, documents are handed over and the acceptance protocol is signed jointly.

Working in live depots

Installing without stopping the workshop

Many installations take place in depots that must keep running. Building a pit under an operational road, diverting cables or bringing heavy assemblies through a busy shed all need careful planning with your traffic and safety teams.

We agree work windows, isolation procedures and lifting plans in advance, and keep the footprint of our activities as small as possible. Where a complete facility is involved, our Turnkey (EPC) Projects team can take responsibility for civil works and interfaces as well. Moving an existing machine to a new location is covered by our SPM relocation and shifting service.
