Products

Railway machine tools for every stage of the wheelset's life

From re-profiling wheels in service to building new wheelsets and keeping depots productive, HYT designs and builds machines around the realities of railway maintenance.

Our range

Engineered for the wheelset, built for the workshop

Every railway vehicle depends on its wheelsets. Their profile governs ride quality, rail wear and derailment safety; their geometry decides whether a train may run at all. HYT Engineering has concentrated on this subject since 1977, and the product range reflects it: machines that restore, build, measure and balance wheelsets, plus the depot equipment that keeps maintenance flowing.

Every machine is designed in-house in Pune and adapted to the customer's track gauge, wheel profiles and vehicle types. Where one machine is not enough, we combine several into a complete facility through our turnkey projects – and support them for their working life through our service organisation.

  • Wheel lathes that re-profile wheelsets in or out of the vehicle
  • Wheel and axle shop machines for new builds and overhaul
  • Depot equipment for armatures, train washing and shunting
  • Partner technology from Germany, distributed exclusively in India

Decision help

How to choose the right wheel lathe

The right machine depends less on the wheel than on your operation. Six questions narrow the choice quickly.

Does the wheelset stay in the vehicle?

To re-profile without dropping bogies, choose an underfloor lathe. If wheelsets are removed anyway during overhaul, a surface or portal lathe fits that flow.

How many wheelsets per day?

Fleet size and re-profiling interval set the workload. High-utilisation metro and EMU fleets often justify a tandem machine that handles a whole bogie in one setting.

What civil works are possible?

Underfloor lathes need a reinforced pit beneath the track. Surface and portal lathes work on removed wheelsets and need no pit under a running line – often simpler in an existing building.

Which wheel sizes and gauges?

List the smallest and largest tread diameters, axle lengths and gauges in your fleet, including vehicles you expect to buy. The machine is sized to that envelope.

Brake discs as well?

Wheel- and axle-mounted brake discs can be machined on suitable configurations. Tell us early, because it affects tooling and machine layout.

Stand-alone or part of a line?

Linked with measuring stations and wheelset handling, a lathe can become part of an automated wheel shop. Plan the interfaces from the start.
