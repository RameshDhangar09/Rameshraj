Retro-fitment and upgradation of wheel lathes

A well-built machine structure can outlast its control system by decades. Retro-fitment replaces what is obsolete and keeps what is sound, giving you a modern machine for a fraction of the disruption of a new one.

Why retrofit

Keep the structure, renew the intelligence

Machine beds, frames and slideways are built to last. Control systems, drives and electronics are not: within a decade or two, components are discontinued, spares become hard to source and every breakdown takes longer to fix. At the same time, newer controls offer better diagnostics, easier programming and profile measurement that saves wheel material.

Retro-fitment addresses both problems. We assess the machine, keep the parts that are still sound and replace the systems that limit availability or performance. For an underfloor lathe, it also avoids the civil works and long shutdown that a new pit machine would require.

Our first concern is HYT machines, whose designs we know in detail. If your machine is from another manufacturer, send us the details and we will tell you honestly whether an assessment makes sense.

What can be upgraded

Typical retro-fitment packages

CNC system

A current industrial CNC with railway-specific cycles, profile libraries and on-screen diagnostics.

Drives and motors

New servo and main drives for reliable performance and available spares.

Measuring system

Automatic wheel profile measurement so only the necessary material is removed.

Hydraulics

Overhauled or replaced power packs, valves and cylinders.

Safety

Updated guarding, interlocks and emergency-stop circuits to current practice.

Data and reporting

Machining reports and measurement records that can be exported for fleet maintenance systems.

Signs that a machine is ready for retro-fitment

  • Control or drive spares are discontinued or hard to source
  • Breakdowns take longer to diagnose because diagnostics are limited
  • The machine cannot store or measure the profiles your fleet now needs
  • Measurement data has to be recorded by hand
  • Guarding or interlocks no longer meet your safety standards
  • The structure and geometry are still sound

Our process

How a retro-fitment project runs

  1. 01

    Condition assessment

    Our engineers inspect the structure, geometry, hydraulics and electrics and review the machine's history.

  2. 02

    Proposal

    We recommend a scope – from a single system to a full rebuild – with the benefits and risks of each option.

  3. 03

    Engineering

    New electrical, hydraulic and software designs are prepared and components procured before the shutdown.

  4. 04

    Shutdown and execution

    The machine is taken out of service only when parts are ready, keeping downtime as short as possible.

  5. 05

    Re-commissioning

    Geometry, measuring and machining are proven on your wheelsets against agreed criteria.

  6. 06

    Training and handover

    Operators and maintainers are trained on the new systems and documentation is updated.
