About the machine

Restoring the commutator, keeping motors in service

In a DC traction motor, current reaches the armature through the commutator and carbon brushes. Over time the commutator wears out of round, its surface roughens and the mica insulation between the copper segments ends up level with the copper. The results are rapid brush wear, sparking and, eventually, motor failure.

Restoring the commutator involves several operations: turning the surface true, undercutting the mica below the copper and finishing the segment edges. The multipurpose armature machine performs these operations on one machine, so the armature moves between workstations less often.

The exact range of functions is configured to the armature types in your fleet.

Key advantages

Why combine armature operations

Several operations, one machine

Turning, undercutting and related work without moving the armature between machines.

True-running commutator

A concentric commutator surface supports even brush contact.

Clean undercut

Mica cut back below the copper to prevent brush wear and flashover.

Less handling

Heavy armatures are lifted and set up fewer times.

Adaptable

Configured to the armature sizes in your fleet.

Depot-friendly

One machine instead of several saves floor space in the workshop.

Process

Typical armature overhaul sequence

  1. 01

    Load

    The armature is lifted into the machine and supported.

  2. 02

    Check run-out

    The commutator is checked for eccentricity and surface condition.

  3. 03

    Turn

    The commutator surface is turned true.

  4. 04

    Undercut

    The mica between segments is cut back below the copper.

  5. 05

    Finish

    Segment edges are chamfered and the surface cleaned.

  6. 06

    Inspect

    The armature is checked before reassembly into the motor.
