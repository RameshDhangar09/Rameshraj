About the machine

The first precision step of a new wheelset

Before a wheel can be pressed on to an axle, its hub bore must be machined to an exact diameter for the interference fit, and its tread and flange must match the specified profile. A vertical turning and boring machine is the natural tool for this work: the wheel lies on a horizontal rotary table, is clamped and machined from above.

The vertical layout makes heavy wheels easy to load by crane, keeps the workpiece stable under cutting load and gives the operator a clear view of the cutting zone. The same machine handles tyres for tyred wheels and other round components of the wheelset.

HYT has supplied a CNC vertical turning lathe to the Sanpada EMU car shed in Mumbai, and a CNC vertical turret lathe to the State Railway of Thailand in Bangkok. See our executed projects.

Key advantages

Why machine wheels vertically

Accurate hub bores

Bore diameter and finish controlled for a reliable interference fit on the axle.

Stable clamping

The wheel lies flat on the table, so its own weight helps it seat firmly.

Fewer re-clampings

Several features machined in one set-up reduce handling and accumulated errors.

Easy crane loading

Heavy wheels and tyres are lowered straight on to the table.

Programs per wheel drawing

Stored CNC programs keep every wheel of a type identical.

New build and overhaul

Suited to new wheels, tyres and overhaul work in the same shop.

Machining process

From rough wheel to finished bore

  1. 01

    Load

    The wheel or tyre is lowered on to the rotary table by crane.

  2. 02

    Centre & clamp

    It is centred and clamped for machining.

  3. 03

    Turn the profile

    Tread, flange and rim faces are turned to the drawing.

  4. 04

    Bore the hub

    The hub bore is machined to the diameter needed for the press fit.

  5. 05

    Face & finish

    Hub faces, chamfers and transitions are finished.

  6. 06

    Inspect

    Critical dimensions are checked before the wheel goes to the press.
