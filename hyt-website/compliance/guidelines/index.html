Compliance

Business Conduct Guidelines

These guidelines turn the principles of our Code of Conduct into practical rules for everyday situations in sales, purchasing, projects and service.

1. How to use these guidelines

These guidelines support the Code of Conduct and apply to the same people: everyone working for HYT, and partners acting on our behalf. If a situation is not covered, ask yourself: Is it legal? Is it consistent with the Code? Would I be comfortable if it were reported to our customer or published? If the answer to any question is no, or you are unsure, ask your manager before acting.

2. Gifts and hospitality

Modest gifts and hospitality can support good business relationships, but they must never create an obligation or the appearance of one.

  • Accept or offer only items of modest value, such as promotional items, normal business meals or festival sweets, within limits set by management.
  • Never give or accept cash, cash equivalents, vouchers, loans or personal favours.
  • Do not offer gifts or hospitality to customer staff involved in a current tender, inspection or acceptance decision.
  • Respect the rules of the recipient's organisation – many public bodies prohibit gifts entirely.
  • Record gifts and hospitality above the agreed threshold and inform your manager.

3. Working with public customers and tenders

Many of our customers are public railway and metro authorities. Their procurement rules exist to ensure fairness and value for public money, and we respect them fully.

  • Submit truthful, complete tender documents and technical data.
  • Comply with integrity pacts, pre-bid procedures and communication rules set by the customer.
  • Do not seek or use confidential information about a tender or a competitor's bid.
  • Do not discuss employment opportunities with public officials involved in decisions affecting HYT.
  • Report any request for an improper payment immediately and refuse it.

4. Agents, consultants and partners

  • Appoint intermediaries only for genuine, documented services and after background checks.
  • Use written contracts that include anti-bribery and compliance clauses.
  • Pay fees that are proportionate to the work, into accounts in the name of the contracting party and in the country where services are performed.
  • Watch for warning signs such as requests for unusual payments, cash, or secrecy.

5. Purchasing and suppliers

  • Select suppliers on quality, delivery, price, safety and ethical performance.
  • Treat all suppliers fairly and keep their quotations confidential.
  • Declare any personal relationship with a supplier and step back from the decision.
  • Expect suppliers to meet the standards of our Code of Conduct; new suppliers can register through Supplier Registration.

6. Exports, sanctions and customs

Machines, spare parts, software and technical data may be subject to export control, sanctions and customs rules in India and in other countries. Check end-use, end-user and destination before quoting or shipping to a new customer or country, declare goods accurately to customs, and seek advice if anything is unclear.

7. Conduct on customer sites

  • Follow the customer's safety rules, permit-to-work systems and track-access procedures without exception.
  • Wear the required personal protective equipment and use only approved tools and lifting equipment.
  • Treat customer premises, equipment and information with care.
  • Report incidents, near misses and damage immediately to the customer and to HYT.

8. Records, documents and reports

  • Record every transaction accurately and on time.
  • Never alter inspection results, test reports, timesheets or expense claims.
  • Keep documents for the periods required by law and by contract.
  • Do not destroy documents that may be relevant to an audit, investigation or legal proceeding.

9. Communication and social media

  • Only authorised people may speak to the media or publish statements on behalf of HYT.
  • Do not post confidential information, customer details, site photographs or drawings on social media without approval.
  • Make clear that personal opinions are your own.

10. IT and information security

  • Use company IT systems for business purposes and in line with company rules.
  • Protect passwords and do not share accounts.
  • Be alert to phishing and report suspicious messages.
  • Connect to customer machines and networks only with authorisation and approved tools.

11. Asking for advice and reporting concerns

If you are unsure what to do, ask your manager, the HR department or senior management. Concerns about possible breaches can be raised through the channels described in section 12 of the Code of Conduct. Nobody will suffer for raising a concern in good faith.
