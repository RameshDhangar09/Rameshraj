Services

Machine relocation and shifting

Moving a wheel lathe or other special purpose machine (SPM) is a project, not a lift. We dismantle, move, re-install and re-commission it so that it performs at the new site as it did at the old one.

Why it needs specialists

Precision machines do not travel well on their own

Depots are rebuilt, fleets move to new sheds and workshops are reorganised. When that happens, a special purpose machine such as a wheel lathe, wheel press or vertical turning machine often moves with them. These machines are heavy, precisely aligned and, in the case of underfloor lathes, built into the track.

A poorly planned move risks damaged assemblies, lost alignment and a long, uncertain re-commissioning. HYT plans each relocation from start to finish: we record the machine's condition and accuracy before dismantling, prepare the new foundation drawings, and re-commission the machine to the same acceptance criteria at its new home.

Relocation is also a good moment to fit upgrades. Many customers combine a move with a retro-fitment of the control system or an overhaul of worn assemblies.

Our process

How we relocate a machine

  1. 01

    Survey

    Inspection of the machine and both sites, with a record of condition and accuracy before the move.

  2. 02

    New site preparation

    Foundation or pit drawings issued for the new location, and readiness checked before dismantling begins.

  3. 03

    Dismantling

    Assemblies are separated, marked and documented in a planned sequence.

  4. 04

    Packing and transport

    Precision surfaces are protected and assemblies are packed and secured for the route.

  5. 05

    Re-installation

    The machine is placed, leveled, aligned and reconnected at the new site.

  6. 06

    Re-commissioning

    Trials on your wheelsets prove that the machine meets its acceptance criteria.

What you gain

Controlled risk at every stage

Documented baseline

Before-and-after accuracy records prove the machine performs as it did.

Planned downtime

A clear schedule agreed with you so that workshop capacity can be planned around the move.

Upgrade opportunity

Combine the move with an overhaul or retro-fitment while the machine is out of service.
