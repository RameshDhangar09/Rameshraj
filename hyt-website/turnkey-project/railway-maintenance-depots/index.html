Turnkey Solutions

Turnkey equipment for railway maintenance depots

A depot earns its keep in train availability. HYT equips the wheel lathe road, washing line, shunting and component work as one project, so trains spend less time waiting and more time in service.

The facility

Built around the hours a train is out of service

Locomotive sheds, coaching depots and EMU or metro car sheds keep fleets fit for daily service. Trains arrive for scheduled examinations, wheel re-profiling, cleaning and repairs, and every hour spent inside the depot is an hour they cannot run. The design of the depot determines how many of those hours are really necessary.

Depot managers know the typical pressures: a single lathe road that turns into a queue, shunting moves that wait for a locomotive and crew, exterior cleaning done by hand, and traction-motor repairs sent away because the shed lacks the right machine. Each problem may sit with a different supplier.

HYT brings the main depot systems under one contract and plans them against your maintenance schedule, track layout and fleet mix. The aim is simple: shorter, more predictable visits for every train.

What HYT delivers

Depot systems under one contract

Wheel re-profiling road

Single or tandem underfloor wheel lathes in a pit, sized to your fleet and the number of axles per visit.

Pits and track interface

Lathe pits, inspection pit lines and rail support coordinated with civil works and the depot track plan.

Train washing

Drive-through washing plant placed on the inbound route, so trains are cleaned without extra moves.

Shunting

Rail cum Road Vehicle (Shunter) for moves between the lathe road, washing line, pit lines and stabling.

Traction-motor work

Armature machining for DC traction motors, carried out in the depot instead of at a distant workshop.

Availability-focused support

Maintenance contracts that keep depot machines ready whenever trains are scheduled to use them.

Depot flow

A train's visit to the depot

  1. 01

    Arrival and washing

    Inbound trains pass through the automated train washing plant on their way to the examination roads.

  2. 02

    Inspection on pit lines

    Running gear, brakes and underframe equipment are examined from pits; wheel defects found here are booked for the lathe.

  3. 03

    Wheel re-profiling

    The train is drawn over the underfloor wheel lathe; worn wheelsets are measured and re-profiled without dropping the bogie.

  4. 04

    Component repairs

    Exchanged traction-motor armatures are restored on the multipurpose armature machine and returned to the store.

  5. 05

    Shunting and stabling

    The Rail cum Road Vehicle (Shunter) moves vehicles between roads and out to the stabling sidings.

  6. 06

    Release to traffic

    Work records are closed and the train returns to the timetable.

Delivering a depot project

Depots are often built or extended while the fleet keeps running. Our Turnkey (EPC) Projects approach is adapted to that reality.

  • Maintenance-led planning: examination intervals, re-profiling frequency and fleet growth define the number of lathe roads, the washing capacity and the shunting needs.
  • Pit and track interface: pit drawings, anchor details, drainage and rail levels issued to the civil contractor, with readiness checked before installation.
  • Installation and commissioning: each system proven on your own vehicles – see Installation & Commissioning.
  • Crew training: lathe operators, shunter drivers and maintenance staff trained on site through our Training programme.
  • Lifecycle care: an Annual Maintenance Contract, a Comprehensive Maintenance Contract (CMC) or full Operation & Maintenance of the depot machines.

Outcomes

What the depot owner gains

More trains in service

Re-profiling without bogie removal and fewer waiting moves shorten every depot visit.

Safer depot routines

Less manual cleaning at height and fewer locomotive shunting moves around staff working on the tracks.

One accountable partner

Lathe, washing, shunting and armature equipment supported by the company that supplied it.
