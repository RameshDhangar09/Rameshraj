Turnkey Solutions

Turnkey wheel shops – from worn wheelset to certified release

A wheel shop decides how quickly worn wheelsets return to traffic. HYT plans, equips and connects the complete overhaul flow – measuring, machining, pressing and balancing – as one coordinated, traceable process.

The facility

Where worn wheelsets become serviceable again

In a railway workshop or periodic overhaul (POH) shop, the wheel shop receives wheelsets that have reached their wear limits, suffered damage or fallen due for scheduled overhaul. Some need only a new profile; others need new wheels, reconditioned journals or a fresh press-fit.

Owners rarely struggle with a single machine. The difficulty lies in the flow: wheelsets queuing at the press while the lathe stands idle, measurements keyed in twice, cranes shared between too many stations. HYT designs the shop around your wheelset mix and overhaul cycle, and builds the core machines itself – so process and equipment are engineered together and one organisation answers for the result.

  1. 01

    Re-profile

    Wheelsets with serviceable wheels receive a new profile with the minimum material removal, so the wheels last more cycles.

  2. 02

    Re-wheel

    Condemned wheels are pressed off, replacement wheels bored to suit the axle seat and pressed on with a recorded force curve.

  3. 03

    Recondition & release

    Journals are restored, wheelsets balanced where specified, and each one leaves with a complete record of what was done.

Inside the wheel shop

The stations of an HYT wheel shop

Each station is an HYT machine designed to work with the next one. Select a station to see its role in the flow.

Measuring & inspection

Every wheelset is measured on arrival and again before release. The wheel measuring station records profile, diameters and back-to-back distance – the data that decides between re-profiling and re-wheeling.

  • Decision point for each wheelset's route
  • Results stored against the wheelset identity
  • Final check before return to service

Wheel re-profiling

Retained wheelsets get a new tread and flange profile on a CNC surface wheel lathe or a portal wheel lathe, chosen to suit your wheelset types and typical defects.

  • Profile programs for your fleet
  • Measurement before the cut to limit material removal
  • Floor-level or portal designs

Wheel boring

Replacement wheels are turned and their bores machined to match the axle seat on a CNC vertical turning and boring machine.

  • Bore sized to the measured axle seat
  • Disc, tyre and hub machining
  • Suits new-build and overhaul work

Axle & journal machining

Axles are restored on an axle journal turning and burnishing lathe; new axles are machined on an axle turning lathe.

  • Journals turned and burnished for durable bearing seats
  • Wheel seats prepared for pressing
  • Axles traceable through the shop

Pressing

A wheel press removes condemned wheels and mounts new ones, with the press-fit force curve recorded for every wheel.

  • Dismounting and mounting on one machine
  • Recorded force curve for each press-fit
  • Data linked to the wheelset record

Balancing & release

Where specified, a dynamic wheel balancing machine checks and corrects imbalance before bearings and axleboxes are refitted and the wheelset is released.

  • Imbalance measured and corrected
  • Final inspection recorded
  • Complete record travels with the wheelset

Process flow

A wheelset's route through the overhaul shop

The sequence below is typical. The actual route for each wheelset depends on the condition found at inspection.

  1. 01

    Receipt and cleaning

    Wheelsets are identified and cleaned so that wear and defects can be seen and measured.

  2. 02

    Inspection and measurement

    Profile, diameters and back-to-back distance are recorded on a wheel measuring station; the result decides between re-profiling and re-wheeling.

  3. 03

    Dismounting

    Where wheels are condemned, a wheel press removes them from axles that will be reused.

  4. 04

    Axle and wheel machining

    Journals are restored on an axle journal turning and burnishing lathe; replacement wheels are bored to suit the axle seat on a vertical turning and boring machine.

  5. 05

    Pressing and re-profiling

    New wheels are pressed on with a recorded force curve; retained wheelsets are re-profiled on a surface or portal wheel lathe.

  6. 06

    Balancing, reassembly and release

    Where specified, wheelsets are balanced. Bearings and axleboxes are refitted, a final check is recorded and the wheelset returns to service.

Wheel shop automation

From separate machines to one connected process

In many wheel shops each machine works well on its own, but the process between them does not. Heavy wheelsets are moved by crane and hand, measurements are written down and re-entered, and matching a wheel bore to an axle seat depends on careful paperwork. Every manual step costs time and adds a chance of error on a safety-critical component.

Automation links measuring, machining, pressing and balancing so that wheelsets move safely between stations, data passes directly from one machine to the next, and every wheel and axle can be traced from receipt to release. It can be introduced in stages – connect the machines and their data first, then add handling as throughput grows – while the shop keeps producing.

Building blocks

Four elements of an automated wheel shop

Automated handling

Wheelsets and components transferred between stations with less crane work and manual lifting.

Integrated measurement

Wheels, axles and wheelsets measured at defined points, with results stored automatically.

Data flow between machines

Measured values passed directly to the next machine – for example, bore and seat data used for pressing.

Traceability

Each component identified, with its measuring, machining and pressing records kept together.

Project delivery

How we deliver a wheel shop

A wheel shop is often rebuilt inside a working workshop. Our Turnkey (EPC) Projects approach plans every phase around the overhaul programme that must continue.

  1. 01

    Study and layout

    Overhaul volumes, wheelset types and the existing building are analysed and a layout agreed.

  2. 02

    Civil interface

    Foundation and utility data are issued early, so civil work is ready when machines arrive.

  3. 03

    Phased installation

    Stations are replaced or added in stages while the shop keeps working. See Installation & Commissioning.

  4. 04

    Commissioning on your wheelsets

    Each station, then the complete flow, is proven on your own wheelsets against agreed criteria.

  5. 05

    Training and support

    Crews are trained on machines and process through our Training programme; an Annual Maintenance Contract can follow handover.

Outcomes

What a well-planned wheel shop gives you

The value of a wheel shop is measured outside it: in vehicles that return to traffic on time and in wheels that run more kilometres between interventions.

  • Shorter time between a wheelset arriving and its release
  • Minimum material removal, so wheels survive more re-profiling cycles before replacement
  • Documented press-fits and measurements for every overhauled wheelset
  • A complete, traceable record for every wheel and axle
  • Less crane work and manual handling around heavy components
  • One partner accountable for machines, layout and performance
