Turnkey Projects

Turnkey (EPC) Projects for wheel shops and depots

When a railway or metro operator needs a complete wheel shop or depot facility, HYT takes responsibility from first layout to running workshop – one contract, one schedule, one accountable partner.

What turnkey means

One partner from empty shed to running workshop

A wheel shop or depot is more than a collection of machines. Wheelsets must flow from arrival to release without bottlenecks, pits and foundations must match machine designs to the millimetre, and power, compressed air and data must reach the right place at the right time. When these pieces are bought from different suppliers, the gaps between them become the client's problem.

In a turnkey – or EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) – project, HYT takes responsibility for those gaps. We study your fleet and throughput, design the process and layout, supply and integrate the machines, coordinate or execute the civil interface, and commission the complete facility on your own wheelsets.

Because we design and build the core machines ourselves, the process, the foundations and the equipment are engineered together from the start. That reduces interface risk, shortens commissioning and gives you a single point of accountability for performance.

Typical scope

What a turnkey wheel shop or depot project can include

Every project is scoped to the client's needs. The elements below can be combined as required.

Process and layout engineering

Fleet analysis, capacity planning and a wheelset flow that avoids bottlenecks and unnecessary handling.

Civil and building interface

Pit, foundation and rail-support design, coordinated with your civil contractor or executed within our scope.

Machines

Wheel lathes, vertical turning machines, axle lathes, wheel presses, measuring stations and balancing machines.

Wheelset handling

Transfer and handling arrangements that move heavy wheelsets safely between workstations.

Utilities and integration

Power distribution, compressed air, chip handling and data connections planned as one system.

Commissioning and support

Facility-level acceptance, staff training and long-term maintenance contracts.

Project phases

How a turnkey project is delivered

Each phase ends with a clear deliverable that you review and approve before the next begins.

  1. 01

    Requirement study

    Fleet mix, wheel and axle types, throughput targets and site constraints are analysed with your team.

  2. 02

    Concept and layout

    Process flow, machine selection and building layout are developed and agreed.

  3. 03

    Detailed engineering

    Machine designs, pit and foundation drawings, utilities and interfaces are finalised.

  4. 04

    Manufacturing and procurement

    Machines are built and tested at our works in Pune; bought-out equipment is sourced and inspected.

  5. 05

    Site preparation

    Civil works are executed or supervised, and readiness is checked before machines are shipped.

  6. 06

    Installation and integration

    Machines, handling and utilities are installed, connected and tested as a system.

  7. 07

    Commissioning and acceptance

    The facility is proven on your own wheelsets against agreed performance criteria.

  8. 08

    Training, handover and support

    Staff are trained, documentation is handed over and long-term maintenance begins.

Why it matters

Fewer interfaces, less risk, earlier output

Most delays in workshop projects happen at interfaces: a pit that does not match the machine, a power supply that arrives late, a handling system that cannot reach a workstation. A single turnkey partner designs those interfaces out before construction begins.

For railways, metros and rolling stock manufacturers, the result is a facility that reaches production sooner and performs as planned – with one organisation answerable for it.

  • Process, foundations and machines engineered together
  • One programme and one point of contact for the whole facility
  • Lifetime support from the company that built the machines
