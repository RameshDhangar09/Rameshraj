About the vehicle

Shunting without tying up a locomotive

Depots and workshops move vehicles all day: on to the wheel lathe road, through the washing plant, over inspection pits and out to the stabling sidings. Using a locomotive for each move ties up traction and crew, and a locomotive cannot leave the rails to reach a different track.

A Rail cum Road Vehicle (Shunter) can. It drives on the road on its tyres, sets down its rail guide wheels at a track access point and then runs on the rails to couple and move rolling stock. When the job is done, it raises the rail wheels and drives to the next track.

Coupling system, braking, hauling capacity and gauge are matched to the vehicles you need to move and the layout of your depot.

Operating principle

A typical shunting move

  1. 01

    Drive to the track

    The vehicle travels by road to a track access point.

  2. 02

    Rail on

    Rail guide wheels are lowered and the vehicle is aligned on the track.

  3. 03

    Couple

    It couples to the rolling stock and the brake connection is made.

  4. 04

    Shunt

    The consist is moved to the workshop road, wash plant or siding.

  5. 05

    Uncouple

    Brakes are secured on the stock and the vehicle uncouples.

  6. 06

    Rail off

    Rail wheels are raised and the vehicle drives on to its next job.
