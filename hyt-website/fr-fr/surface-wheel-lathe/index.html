About the machine

Re-profiling at floor level

Many workshops remove wheelsets from the vehicle anyway – during bogie overhaul, after a wheel change or when axleboxes and bearings are serviced. For them, machining in a pit beneath the track brings no benefit. A surface wheel lathe does the job at floor level, where the wheelset is easy to reach, inspect and handle.

The wheelset is rolled into the machine on rails, raised into the machining position and clamped. Both wheels are measured and turned to the specified profile, and suitable configurations also machine wheel- or axle-mounted brake discs in the same setting.

Surface or underfloor? An underfloor wheel lathe re-profiles wheels while the wheelset stays in the vehicle. That saves dismantling, but it needs a pit under the track. A surface lathe needs no pit and suits workshops where wheelsets come out of the vehicle in the normal course of maintenance, or where the building does not allow civil works beneath the rails.

Key advantages

Why workshops choose a surface lathe

No pit required

Installed on a level foundation at floor level, which simplifies civil works and suits existing buildings.

Wheels and brake discs

Suitable configurations machine brake discs in the same setting as the wheel profile – the YSL-08 from 250 to 700 mm in diameter.

Open access

The whole wheelset is in view and within reach for inspection, measurement and tool changes.

Measured before cutting

Wheel dimensions are recorded first, so the CNC removes only the material needed to restore the profile.

Broad wheelset envelope

The YSL-08 takes axle lengths from 1200 to 2750 mm, depending on gauge – useful for workshops serving mixed fleets.

Power to suit your wheels

Two main drive options on the YSL-08, so cutting power can be matched to wheel hardness and typical wear.

Model range

YSL-08

The YSL-08 is the reference model of the range. It takes wheelsets with tread diameters up to 1250 mm, wheel widths of 75–145 mm and a weight of up to 50 kN, and machines brake discs from 250 to 700 mm in diameter. A main drive of 2 × 45 kW or 2 × 55 kW and cutting speeds of 25–80 m/min let you match the machine to the wheels you re-profile most often.

Model range

YSL-12

The YSL-12 is a further model in the surface wheel lathe range. Its capacities and configuration are defined with you during the enquiry and confirmed in the technical offer. Share the largest, smallest and heaviest wheelsets in your fleet and we will tell you whether the YSL-12 is the right fit.

Model range

YSL-21

The YSL-21 completes the range. As with the YSL-12, technical data is provided on request. Tell us which vehicles and wheel profiles the machine must cover, and we will send the data together with a model recommendation for your workshop.

Operating principle

How a removed wheelset is re-profiled

  1. 01

    Roll in

    The removed wheelset is rolled along the workshop track into the machine.

  2. 02

    Lift & clamp

    It is raised to the machining position and clamped securely.

  3. 03

    Measure

    Diameter, flange height and flange thickness are recorded on both wheels.

  4. 04

    Program

    The operator selects the target profile and the CNC calculates the cut required.

  5. 05

    Turn

    Tread and flange are machined, followed by brake discs where the configuration includes them.

  6. 06

    Check & roll out

    The wheelset is re-measured, the result documented and the wheelset rolled out for assembly.

Main features

  • Floor-level machine on a level foundation – no track pit
  • Wheelset loading on rails for safe, controlled handling
  • Tool slides for the left and right wheels
  • Brake-disc machining on suitable configurations
  • Profile library stored in the CNC for mixed fleets
  • Choice of main drive power on the YSL-08
  • Chip removal from the cutting zone
  • Guarded working area with a clear operator view

Customer applications

Where a surface lathe makes sense

Locomotive workshops

Re-profiling wheelsets during periodic overhaul, when bogies are already stripped.

Coach and EMU overhaul

Wheel and brake-disc machining in one setting on removed wheelsets.

Wagon repair shops

Removing flats and hollow wear from wagon wheelsets between overhauls.

Workshops without a pit

Buildings where civil works beneath the track are impractical or too costly.

Service and support

From layout to lifetime service

Because the machine stands at floor level, installation is simpler to plan than for a pit lathe. HYT supplies the foundation drawing, checks the workshop layout for wheelset access and craneage, and commissions the lathe on your own wheelsets.

Operators and maintainers are trained on site. Afterwards, spare supply, an Annual Maintenance Contract and retro-fitment keep the machine productive for many years.
